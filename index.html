Physics Explained

Einstein's general relativity describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime. Near a black hole, gravitational time dilation becomes especially significant.

1. Schwarzschild Radius

Rs = 2GM / c2

The Schwarzschild radius determines the event horizon of a non-rotating black hole. It increases proportionally with mass.

G = Gravitational constant
M = Black hole mass
c = Speed of light

2. Gravitational Time Dilation

Δτ / Δt = √(1 − Rs / r)

A stationary clock near a black hole runs slower relative to a clock far away.

Δτ = Time measured near the black hole
Δt = Time measured far away
r = Radial coordinate from the black hole

3. Why Mass Cancels Out

r = kRs
Δτ / Δt = √(1 − 1/k)

When distance is measured as a multiple of Schwarzschild radii, the mass cancels out. At the same value of k, black holes of different masses produce the same time dilation factor.

Model Assumptions and Limitations

This simulator uses the Schwarzschild model, which describes a non-rotating, uncharged black hole.

The equation compares stationary observers outside the event horizon with an observer very far from the black hole.

It does not describe freely falling observers, orbiting spacecraft, or rotating black holes. The visualization is schematic and not to scale.

How to Use This Lab

Explore gravitational time dilation through interactive simulations and comparisons. Follow the steps below to investigate how gravity affects the passage of time.

01

Adjust Your Distance

Use the distance slider to move the observer closer to or farther from the black hole. Distance is measured in Schwarzschild radii (Rs).

02

Compare the Clocks

Press Start to compare two clocks: one near the black hole and one far away. Notice how the difference grows over time.

03

Explore Black Hole Mass

Change the black hole mass using the logarithmic slider in the Comparison section. Observe how the Schwarzschild radius changes while the time dilation factor at 2 Rs remains constant.

04

Understand the Physics

Visit Physics Explained to learn how Schwarzschild radius and gravitational time dilation are calculated. Review the model assumptions to understand what the simulation represents.
